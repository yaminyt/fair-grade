Used iPhone 13 vs iPhone 14: is the newer one worth it?

The two phones look almost identical. Here is what the iPhone 14's extra $40 or so actually buys you.

Prices as of November 2026

The short answer

For most people the iPhone 13 is the better used buy. Both phones have the same 6.1-inch OLED screen, the same A15 Bionic chip family, the same IP68 water resistance and a nearly identical design. The iPhone 14 adds two safety features, a slightly better camera and one extra hour of rated battery life. If those matter to you, the gap in price is small enough to justify it. If not, keep the money.

What they cost used

Swappa's October 2026 average sale prices put a used iPhone 13 at $245 to $347 depending on storage ($245 for 128GB, $259 for 256GB, $347 for 512GB). A used iPhone 14 averaged $284 to $364, again depending on storage. At the entry level that's a difference of roughly $40.

What the iPhone 14 adds

Safety features. Crash Detection is supported on iPhone 14 and later, so the iPhone 13 doesn't have it. The iPhone 14 also has Emergency SOS via satellite, which lets you reach emergency services when you have no cell signal. If you drive a lot or spend time outside coverage, these are the strongest reasons to pay more.

Camera. The main camera has a slightly wider ƒ/1.5 aperture, and the front camera gains autofocus and a wider ƒ/1.9 aperture. Cinematic mode goes from 1080p to 4K HDR, and Action mode (up to 2.8K at 60 fps) is on the iPhone 14's spec sheet but not the 13's. These are real but modest upgrades; casual photographers may not notice much.

Graphics. The iPhone 14's A15 has one more GPU core. Day-to-day speed is very similar.

The SIM card catch

Apple's spec page says US iPhone 14 models use eSIM only and don't take a physical SIM card; the SIM tray exists only on models bought outside the US. The iPhone 13 takes a nano-SIM as well as eSIM. If your carrier or plan makes eSIM awkward, or you like moving a SIM between phones, the iPhone 13 is simpler.

Who each phone suits

Pick the iPhone 13 if you want the best value, use a physical SIM, and mostly need a reliable everyday phone with a good camera.

Pick the iPhone 14 if Crash Detection or satellite SOS gives you peace of mind, you take a lot of selfies or video, and an extra $40 or so is easy to absorb.

Whichever you choose, check battery health before you buy. Both phones are several years old now, and a worn battery can erase the price difference. Our pre-purchase checklist covers IMEI, activation lock and payment protection.
